The new novel

Wanda & Me

Speculative literary fiction · 80,000 words · Montreal, the 1980s

Me is never named. Her family’s most lasting gift is a talent for escape, and she will go anywhere a thrill is hiding, usually with a life-defying dose of something in her pocket.

But Me has an edge nobody else can see. Wanda turned up in an empty Christmas box when Me was small and promised never to abandon her. Wanda is invisible and inaudible to everyone else. She whispers in Me’s ear and turns every harrowing situation into a game.

Then Me gets tired. She wants to go to school. She wants to learn how to cook. She meets Bucky, who is learning to work his wheelchair, and she breaks Wanda’s first rule of loyalty.

It is never a good idea to make Wanda angry.

“She is my alter ego and much meaner than I am.”

Elle Woodman wants nothing more than a home of her own on the lonely isle of No Name Key. Then her brutal, drunken husband Billy tracks her down. On the morning of the 1935 Labor Day hurricane, Elle puts him on the last ferry out.

About

I was born in Ottawa, took an MA in creative writing at Concordia University in Montreal, and co-founded It’s a Bunny, one of the internet’s early literary magazines. My stories ran in Moosehead, Paperplates and Matrix. Then I wrote advertising to pay the rent.

In 2009 I moved from Montreal to Key West and started over. No Name Key began as the winning entry in The Studios of Key West’s Writes of Spring contest. Sidetrack Key began with an Anne McKee Artist Fund grant.

Along the way: first prize in the Tennessee Williams short story contest, a Fish Publishing flash fiction shortlist, poems in SoFloJo, and a story in True Tales from The 90-Day Memoir: Mothers.

I once threw a biography of Raymond Carver across the room. I stand by it.
